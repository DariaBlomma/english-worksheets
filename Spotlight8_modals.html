🟪 BLOCK 2 · THE "VS" BATTLES
The most common traps. Choose your fighter wisely.

⚔️ MUST vs HAVE TO

MUST
Internal obligation

The speaker thinks it's important. Personal feeling or strong recommendation.

Example:

I must call my mom. (I feel it's important)

VS
HAVE TO
External obligation

Rules, laws, boss, or situation force you. No personal choice.

Example:

I have to wear a uniform. (It's the school rule)

⚠️ THE NEGATIVE TRAP: They change completely in negative form!
MUSTN'T = It's forbidden! 🚫 (You mustn't smoke here.)
DON'T HAVE TO = It's not necessary. You can if you want. ✅ (You don't have to pay, it's free.)

⚔️ CAN vs BE ABLE TO

CAN
General ability

Use for Present and Past (could). Describes a skill or regular capability.

Limitation: No Future form, no Perfect tenses.

Examples:

I can swim. (Present ability)

I could swim when I was 5. (Past general ability)

VS
BE ABLE TO
Universal backup + Specific achievement

Use when CAN doesn't exist (Future, Perfect) OR for a specific difficult achievement in the past.

Examples:

I will be able to help you tomorrow. (Future - CAN has no future form)

After 3 hours, I was able to fix the computer. (Specific achievement, not "could fix")

🟧 BLOCK 3 · TIME TRAVEL (Perfect Modals)
Modal + HAVE + V3 · How to talk about the past.
Meaning Paraphrase / Trigger Structure Example
Logical Certainty I'm certain that it happened must have + V3 She must have forgotten her keys.
Logical Impossibility it isn't possible that it happened can't have + V3 He can't have stolen it.
Past Possibility it's possible that it happened might/may/could have + V3 They might have missed the bus.
Regret / Criticism it was wrong not to do it should have + V3 You should have revised more.
Wasted Effort it wasn't necessary (but you did it) needn't have + V3 You needn't have cooked dinner.
